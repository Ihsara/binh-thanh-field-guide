Little Tokyo on Phạm Viết Chánh

A few blocks from the Cầu Thị Nghè 2 bridge, places listed as Japanese crowd together.

The listings turn Japanese on Phạm Viết Chánh and Mê Linh, a few blocks from the Thị Nghè canal. In the small circle of streets this map enlarges, 173 places are listed as food or drink, and 44 of them as Japanese; 10 of those carry Japanese script in their listed names. Their stretches of street are red. Each word on the red turns up in the listings of more than one of them: sushi, izakaya, ramen.

13×as common here as across the district: 44 of the district's 104 places listed as Japanese sit in this one small circle of streets

They have company. Around the same blocks, 8 places are listed as Western, and their stretches are teal: the listings include tacos, burgers and French restaurants. Every other listing's stretch is grey, and one of them stands alone on the map: CherryBee IceCream, the only place in the circle filed first as an ice cream shop.

Who eats here? The listings say where the restaurants are, not who sits at the counter. That makes it a question worth walking over to ask. → Three campuses, two pockets, p. 03
