The road before the roads

Historians trace the old Mandarin Road along two of today's streets.

In 1748 a branch of the Thiên Lý, the mandarins' road north, was opened through this ground. Historians trace it from the Thị Nghè bridge up Xô Viết Nghệ Tĩnh, over Cầu Sơn and Cầu Kinh, then along đường Bình Quới; one researcher carries it on to the old Bình Quới ferry on the Saigon River. The line on the map follows those streets.

6.8 kmof today's streets on the line of the 1748 road, from the Thị Nghè bridge to the Bình Quới ferry, where one researcher ends it

The tracing is a reconstruction. Today's streets follow the old line; they are not the old road. The inset is from Trần Văn Học's map, the earliest of Gia Định drawn by a Vietnamese mapmaker, seen in a later reproduction. It marks the road leaving past Cầu Sơn, labelled as the way to Huế and Thăng Long.
