How we know

This atlas reads frozen extracts, never a live map. Each page uses one release of its data and never mixes two, with two kinds of exception. Before the towers and The open peninsula set the old military sheet beside dates from our history ledger. And four pages of the first chapter, Bình Thạnh eats in pockets, Little Tokyo, Little Seoul between two campuses and Three campuses, two pockets, count Overture's listings but name the streets those listings lie on from OpenStreetMap, where the streets are drawn; the last two also walk from a food pocket to campuses drawn on OpenStreetMap, along its streets. Every number you see is computed from a file in this repository, and the table for each map below names that file, the release it comes from and how the number was counted.

Two sources feed the maps of the first chapter. Overture Maps is a commercial-grade open dataset that merges several providers into one list of places, with a category for each, and one set of building footprints. OpenStreetMap is a map drawn by volunteers: streets, wards, rivers, bridges and campuses. Neither is a census, and both miss the stall that has no sign. The last two maps of that chapter look at what that costs. Place data: Overture Maps Foundation, CDLA-Permissive-2.0. Buildings: Overture Maps Foundation, ODbL. Streets and boundaries: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

Where Bình Thạnh Eats. Every count in the first chapter comes from one Overture release: 5,583 places inside the district that it files as food and drink, each sorted into one kind of kitchen by its category. Not all of them are places to eat. Before looking for pockets we flagged every listing whose other categories or name point elsewhere, read each one by hand, and took out 120: a university faculty and a phone shop filed as Korean restaurants, a law firm, a laundry, wholesalers and importers. A listing the flags missed can still be in the counts. A pocket is a circle of 200 m radius, centred on a place, that holds at least 6 places of one kind, where that kind is at least 2× as common as across the district. For each kind we ranked the circles and kept the best ones that do not overlap, then read the names inside to check they match the kind: izakaya in Little Tokyo, mì cay and kimbap in Little Seoul. On the maps, a place colours the street segment nearest to it, if that segment is within 40 m and inside the circle. A place farther from any street colours nothing, so the coloured streets can carry a little less than a pocket's count. What sits next to a pocket (a bridge, a campus, a roundabout, a boulevard) was looked up on OpenStreetMap within 300 m of its centre, and the page names the clearest one. Neither quán chay pocket has a clear one, so neither gets a next-door line.

This edition also fixes four misreadings in how places are sorted into kinds. A name spelled hủ tíu, rather than hủ tiếu, went unrecognised; it counts as hủ tíu now. A name with "Chẩy" in it, as in Quán Hủ Tíu Hùng Chẩy, was read as chay, vegetarian; a name has to say chay itself now, and such shops count as hủ tíu. A cơm chay shop, filed under cơm before, now counts as quán chay. And Overture puts its ramen tag on noodle shops of every kind, which had sent Chinese noodle shops, mì gia and sủi cảo among them, into the Japanese kind; a shop with that tag now counts as Japanese only when its name says so. The pockets were found again on the fixed sorting, and every count on the pocket pages comes from it. The district's hub pages, outside this atlas, were not rebuilt and still use the sorting from before the fix.

The words set along a pocket's red stretches are dish names found in the names and Overture categories of its places of that kind. A word is drawn only when it turns up in at least 2 places, and a pocket carries at most 3, the most frequent first. On the two overview maps a word is drawn only where it fits along its stretch at that screen width, so it can be missing there. The words come from a fixed list and are set in Latin letters, so a listing named 김밥 or gimbap counts as kimbap. The streets named in the text for Little Seoul, Little Tokyo and the café street are the ones whose coloured stretches carry the most listings, at most 2, each stretch matched to the nearest street on OpenStreetMap and each hẻm counted with the street it opens off.

The walking minutes on the campus pages are walked along streets, never measured as the crow flies. From the pocket's centre we step onto the nearest street on OpenStreetMap, which for Little Seoul is Võ Oanh, and follow the street network to a fixed point on each campus at 80 m a minute, rounded to the whole minute. OpenStreetMap maps no entrance for HUTECH or UTH, so each of those walks ends at a point we pinned. HUTECH's walk ends at the Điện Biên Phủ frontage, end of Hẻm 479 (inferred from its address, 475A Điện Biên Phủ; no entrance mapped). UTH's walk ends at the nearest outline point on Võ Oanh (no entrance mapped); it is under a minute and stays on that one street, so its label reads "across the road". Văn Lang's walk ends at an entrance mapped on OpenStreetMap. By HUTECH's outline the close-up writes "Korean studies", our English for its Khoa Hàn Quốc học. The Little Seoul page's Korean studies faculty and Vietnam-Korea institute are likewise our glosses of its Khoa Hàn Quốc học and Viện Công nghệ Việt - Hàn, not the university's own names.

A building or a listing is on an avenue when it stands within 30 m of one, by the hẻm study's road map. Buildings are Overture's footprints of the same release, 44,723 of them, each placed at the centre of its box. Some listings are not kitchens at all: names we read by hand turned up a nail salon, a laundry and a juice brand, at about the same rate on the avenues as in the hẻm. The gap between the two holds, but no share in the chapter is exact, and none of them counts what has no listing.

The block page's square is 300 m on a side, from the district's core, with the Thanh Đa peninsula left out. It had to hold at least 245 buildings, the median for a square, with at least 80% of them set back from the avenues; of those squares it has the fewest listings per building. We read each of its listings by hand, and neither is a kitchen (a house-for-rent advert, a shop selling dried pomelo peel), so the page counts none.

What It Used to Be. The second chapter sets old maps under today's streets. Its main map is the 1984 topographic sheet of the US Defense Mapping Agency, a US federal work in the public domain; the scan is from the University of Texas Libraries. In this part of the city the sheet's drawing does not sit where its own printed grid says it should, missing it by up to 116.8 m. So we registered the sheet to what it draws instead: 25 road junctions and bridge mid-spans, each matched to the same place in OpenStreetMap. Every one of those points was scored by a fit that never saw it, and the median miss is 22.6 m.

The sheet ends before the district does. It holds 94.3% of the district and 81.4% of Phường Bình Quới; the eastern tip of the Thanh Đa peninsula is off its edge, and every share in the chapter is of the part on the sheet. The built-up fill, printed red on the sheet and traced over it on the maps as a solid fill, is found by its colour, checked against squares we labelled by hand. Every colour setting that reads those squares equally well gives a district share between 15.1% and 19.1%, so the headline is a rough share within that band. No page gives a built-up share for today: only the old sheet is measured.

The French survey of 1895 (Joly, Plan des environs de Saïgon) is public domain (copyright expired), via Wikimedia Commons. The fit it was given before misses its check points by a median of 1,218 m, too far to draw under today's streets, so it is shown as a picture. The inset on the road page is Trần Văn Học's map of Gia Định of 1815, photographed from a later printed reproduction (Địa chí Văn hóa Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh), via Wikimedia Commons, where the file is tagged public domain in the US. Credit: Trần Văn Học, Bản đồ Gia Định; photograph uploaded by Bùi Thụy Đào Nguyên (file page). It is illustrative only and is never laid on the map. The line of the Mandarin Road, and the dates on the tower and peninsula pages, come from the sister site's research: the route follows the streets historians name, and each date has two independent sources.

The Alleys. The third chapter reads the address each listing gives in Overture's places, release 2026-10-21.0. A listing counts when the centre of its box lies inside the district's outline from before the 2025 redraw: 29,065 listings. The April-to-October count on the third page also reads the release of 2026-04-15.0. The streets, the alleys and the routes along them are OpenStreetMap's. Like the rest of the atlas, these pages count listings, not what stands in the street; a "door" here is a written address line, not a counted doorway.

Only the first line of an address is read. A listing has a house number when that line starts with one, after an optional "Số"; we read each slash in the number as one more alley in from the street the address names next. A split number has a letter or "bis" (or "ter") in the house or alley number. Where the number is a range, its first number counts (381 listings). The street is the words after the number, up to the first comma, bracket or ward; 57 other spellings of a street, with or without tone marks or "Đường", are folded into its most listed one. Inside old Ward 25, a street written D1, D2 or D3 counts under its new name (175 listings). Not every listing parses: 1,296 give no address, 3,420 give one that does not start with a house number (a building, a floor, a street alone), and 206 give a number but no street we can read. Those last are among the 24,349 numbered listings, so they count in the district's shares, on no street. A few address fragments also read as street names (a "Floor", say); none has enough listings to be printed.

Each listing gets one kind from its Overture category: place of worship, food, shop, office, school, or other. Overture files 194 listings as places of worship. We read each one by hand and moved 30 to other: youth groups and choirs, a diocesan office, a shop selling Buddhist goods, a tomb, a bridge, a yogurt shop. 123 of the rest give a house number; those are the first page's places of worship.

Each route on the first page starts at the nearest point of the street the address names and follows OpenStreetMap's streets and alleys to the point Overture lists. A place further than 60 m from any mapped street gets no route; none was. Where OpenStreetMap has no usable match for the named street, the route starts on the nearest main road (2 drawn routes). Of the 61 places of worship with an alley address, 54 are drawn. Not drawn: 4 whose route is shorter than 5 m, as the point sits on the named street itself (Nhà Thờ Bình Hòa, 93/9, Nơ Trang Long; Chùa Diệu Giác, 248/2, Trần Kế Xương; Chua Chau An, 498/1/2, Lê Quang Định; Chùa An Hòa, 70/1, Quốc Lộ 4, Khu Phố 2); 2 whose named street OpenStreetMap draws only outside the district outline (Chùa Thiền Tịnh, 87/410, Đinh Tiên Hoàng; Chùa Phổ Hiền, 87/44/6 Hẻm 87 Đinh Tiên Hoàng); and 1 whose route is longer than 1,000 m (Chùa Pháp Quang Bình Thạnh, 414/17 Đường Nơ Trang Long). So the Thanh Đa route is the longest drawn way in, not the longest listed. The three places the notes name are Bửu Lâm Tự, 273/29/13/34, Nguyễn Văn Đậu; Chùa Pháp Quang Tự, 482/10/36/10 Hẻm 10 Đường Nơ Trang Long; and Đền Giê-ra-đô, 352/19 Bình Quới, on the Thanh Đa peninsula.

A main street here is any street with at least 30 numbered listings, and a street's split share is printed or painted only then. The second page draws 65 main streets; 8 more have enough listings but are not drawn, because OpenStreetMap does not give them as one street inside the district (numbered streets in several pieces, a name it lacks, a street OpenStreetMap draws only outside the outline). The page was planned to set old Ward 25's D-streets beside the district's other main streets. Before counting, we set the test: the other streets' split share at least 2× the D-streets'. It came out at 1.9× (16% against 8%), so the page leads with the whole district and keeps the D-streets as a note.

The two pages count the D-streets differently. The second counts numbered listings by the street name they write, wherever in the district they lie. The third counts every listing inside old Ward 25 that writes one of the three streets, by either name, with or without a house number. Each gives the old code name only (231), the new name only (1,486), or both (8); a listing that gives both counts as giving the old. 5 listings use D1, D2 or D3 as a block or unit code and are left out. Which code goes with which name is read from the listings: some write both on one line, as in "36 Nguyễn Gia Trí ( D2 cũ ), P.25, Quận Bình Thạnh". No listing writes D3 beside Võ Oanh; of the 29 listings that write D3 as a street, 27 lie nearest Võ Oanh of the three D-streets. Old Ward 25 lies wholly inside Phường Thạnh Mỹ Tây by the ward outlines of the redraw (Nghị quyết 1685/NQ-UBTVQH15), as the sister site draws them.

The ward-naming counts on the third page (the share of doors, and each street's) and every count on the fourth are of doors, not listings. A door is a listing's first address line, with case and spacing evened out; listings that share one count once. One door can carry many listings: 11 listings name Hẻm 24 Bùi Đình Túy, all at one door, 33/4, and 23 listings that give a new ward name share one door, 365 Lê Quang Định; the listing names we read there advertise printing ("In Bạt Hiflex Khổ Lớn Lấy Ngay", "In Folder, Bìa Hồ Sơ Giá Rẻ TPHCM"). Door numbers in the notes are quoted as listed, letters and case included.

That door is why the doors are counted. Overture places it in old Phường 5. Counted by listing, Phường 5 stood out from every other old ward: 31 of its 55 listings that name a ward gave a new name, and 23 of those were that one door. Counted by door, it is one door like any other.

A door gives the old ward when its first line writes a numbered ward (Phường, P. or F and a number); it gives the new ward when it writes one of the five new wards, whatever else it writes. A numbered ward written in English or without tone marks, such as "Ward 22" or "Phuong 25", is not read: 50 listings. A door that writes only the district, Quận Bình Thạnh, names no ward and is left out. A door takes the class of its first listing. 148 listings name a ward of another district and are left out too. The notes give a street's share only on at least 30 doors. The fainter lines are drawn from 10 doors up (24 streets): below the atlas's rule for a printed share, so they carry no number.

The April-to-October count follows listings, not doors, by their Overture id. Of the 981 listings that gave an old ward in April, by October 557 still gave an old ward, 10 gave a new ward name, 152 gave no ward, and 262 were no longer listed under the same id.

26 listings write "cũ", old, into the address, beside an old street name, an old house number, or an old ward or district: "36 Nguyễn Gia Trí ( D2 cũ ), P.25, Quận Bình Thạnh", "744, số cũ/604 Xô Viết Nghệ Tĩnh", "25/15 Nguyễn công trứ, Phường Thạnh Mỹ Tây (P19, Q.Bình Thạnh Cũ)". Some of them are about places in other districts.

On the fourth page, a door gives another alley's number when its first line names a hẻm on a street, as in Hẻm 47/52 Bùi Đình Túy, and its house number, all but the part after the last slash, neither starts with that hẻm's number nor is the start of it. A door whose number starts with the end of the hẻm's number abbreviates the alley's own number and is not counted. Nor is a door whose number, all but the part after the last slash, ends with the hẻm's number: its listing names the hẻm the door is on by that hẻm's last number. A house number with no slash is a house in the hẻm the listing names, read as that alley's own. Listings name 70 alleys where a door gives another alley's number. The map draws the 39 that OpenStreetMap names on the same street. Of the rest, OpenStreetMap names 24 only on other streets and 7 not at all, so they are not drawn.
